Where the units go

    Check these before you rely on the number

    Free units: pages written before May 2026 say 100 free units. For FY 2026-27, secondary sources report 200 free units for homes using up to 500 units in the two months, under G.O.(Ms.) No.50 of 10 May 2026 and TNERC Order No.5 of 2026. Above 500 units only 100 are free. The page uses 200 by default.

    Slab rates: these are from TNERC Tariff Order No.6 of 2025, effective 1 July 2025. An inflation revision was due on 1 July 2026, but pages checked up to October 2026 report that none was published and that no domestic hike was applied. Electricity duty is taken as 5% of the energy charge. Your actual bill can differ because of meter-reading dates and any adjustment shown on it.

    Rates used (you can change them)

    Slab prices are fixed in the code and listed in the table of the result. Edit calc.js to change them when the tariff is revised.

    How this is calculated

    • Units for the cycle = count × watts × hours a day × days ÷ 1000, added up over all appliances.
    • Billing is telescopic: each price applies only to the units inside its slab.
    • At 500 units or fewer, the first 200 units are free. At 501 units or more, only the first 100 are free and every slab above 500 is dearer, so crossing the line adds a large step.
    • The split by appliance is approximate. It divides the bill in proportion to units, which ignores that free units come off the lowest slabs.
