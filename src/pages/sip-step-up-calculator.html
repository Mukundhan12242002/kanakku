Kanakku / SIP step-up

SIP with a yearly step-up, in today's rupees

படிப்படியாக உயரும் SIP

Most people's income rises each year, so a SIP that rises with it grows much more than a flat one. This shows both, and what the final amount is worth after inflation.

What it could grow to

This is arithmetic, not a forecast

Market returns are not steady. Equity funds can lose money over several years, and the return you type in is only an assumption. Try a low figure as well as a high one. Investments are subject to market risk.

How this is calculated

  • One instalment is invested at the start of each month. The monthly return is the yearly figure divided by 12.
  • After each 12 months the instalment is raised by the step-up percentage.
  • "In today's rupees" divides the final value by (1 + inflation) for each year.
  • Taxes, expense ratios and exit loads are not included.

Questions people ask

How much difference does a step-up make?

Over 15 years at 12%, a SIP of ₹10,000 that rises 10% a year ends well above a flat ₹10,000, because the later, larger instalments are added while the early ones keep compounding. Change the step-up to 0 to see the flat figure.

Why show the value after inflation?

₹1 crore in 15 years does not buy what ₹1 crore buys today. Dividing by inflation shows the amount in today's purchasing power, which is the figure to compare with your goal.

What return should I assume?

Nobody knows. Many planners use a range, for example 8%, 10% and 12%, and plan on the lower one. Past fund returns do not guarantee future returns.
